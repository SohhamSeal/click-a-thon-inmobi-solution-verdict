Chapter 5: The Algorithms

Detectors, statistical tests, correction, localization, and confidence. With formulae and alternatives.

Simple first

Two detectors, one job

Verdict asks two different questions about each lattice cell:

DetectorQuestionNeeds history?Catches
TemporalDid this segment change vs its own past?Yes (4 weeks)Main effects (Android 15 outage)
StructuralIs this cell weird vs its row and column siblings?NoInteractions, compensating pairs

Stats primer (minimal)

p value

If nothing was really wrong, how often would we see a move this big (or bigger) by luck? Small p = surprising under "nothing changed." Default threshold after correction: 0.01.

z score

How many "typical noise units" away is the observation from expectation?

z = (observed − expected) / standard_error

relative effect

relative_effect = observed / expected − 1

Example: fill rate 0.68 vs baseline 0.75 → relative effect = −9.3%

Temporal detector

Temporal: tests by metric type

1. Proportion metrics (fill_rate, ctr, render_rate)

Two proportion z test with overdispersion φ inflating variance.

z = (pobs − pbase) / √(φ · p̄(1−p̄) · (1/nobs + 1/nbase))

Why not plain binomial? Ad segments are overdispersed. Binomial would flag everything as significant.

Alternatives considered: Fisher's exact (too slow at scale), beta binomial (more complex, similar spirit).

2. Count metrics (requests, fills, impressions, clicks)

Quasi Poisson test: variance = φ × expected instead of expected.

z = (observed − expected) / √(φ · expected)

Why? Request counts correlate (shared traffic conditions). Pure Poisson understates noise.

3. Ratio metrics (ecpm, rpr)

Log ratio test with Student's t: work in log space, MAD for spread, t distribution with k−1 df.

t = (log(observed) − median(log history))) / (max(σMAD, floor) · √(1 + 1/k))

Why Student's t, not normal? With only 4 history points, treating estimated spread as exact produced absurd p values (e.g. 1.6e−155 for a 5% move). t prices in uncertainty.

Why log? eCPM moves are proportional ("down 10%") not additive ("down $0.50").

Baseline pooling (critical detail)

Expected rate = sum(fills) / sum(requests) over kept weeks. Never average of weekly rates unless volumes match.

Structural detector

Structural: Tukey median polish

For each 2 way combo in the window, fit on log(metric value):

log(v) ≈ grand + row_effect + column_effect

Fit by repeatedly subtracting medians along rows and columns (robust to outliers).

Residual = what's left. Big residual = cell doesn't match what its row and column predict.

Flag if |residual| / sampling_error > z_threshold (default 5.0).

Compensating pair example: Banner eCPM down, Video eCPM up, total flat. Additive fit expects each cell near row+column. Both legs have huge residuals even though total is fine.

Why medians not means? The anomaly would pull the fit toward itself and hide in the residual.

Alternatives: ANOVA on means (not robust), isolation forest (black box, hard to audit), SHAP (needs ML model).

Benjamini Hochberg

False discovery control (temporal only)

Sort p values. Find largest rank k where p(k) ≤ α · k / m. Keep findings 1..k.

m = total hypotheses tested, not just those with small p. Understating m makes correction silently permissive.

Why BH not Bonferroni? Bonferroni is very conservative with thousands of correlated segments. FDR targets expected false discovery rate, better for exploratory monitoring.

Localization

Counterfactual localization

Ranking stopped. Each candidate faces tests that can fail:

TestQuestionPass means
SufficiencyRemove candidate from parent. Does parent return to expected?Candidate explains the move (driver)
MinimalityRemove guiltiest child. Does candidate still deviate?Candidate is not too broad
MaximalityDid siblings move the same way?Candidate is not too narrow
HoldoutEffect in both half windows?Not a one off spike
Android 15 example: Remove Android 15 → global fill rate recovers. Sufficiency passes. Remove Galaxy S23 → almost no recovery. Sufficiency fails. Galaxy S23 is a passenger.

Outcomes: localized (one accused survived all tests), unlocalized (something moved, no candidate survived).

From one 24h run: 287 candidates, only 1 accused. 174 rejected as too narrow, 45 too broad, etc.

Rate / mix decomposition

For ratio metrics, total change splits exactly into three terms:

ΔR = Rate term + Mix term + Interaction term
  • Rate: segment rates changed, shares held at baseline
  • Mix: traffic shares shifted toward segments with different baseline rates
  • Interaction: both moved together

Android 15 incident: ~100% rate term, ~0% mix → real outage, not demand shift.

Confidence

Confidence score (five components)

ComponentWeightMeasures
Significance0.25Strength of statistical evidence (p value in log decades)
Sufficiency0.30How much removing accused restores parent
Minimality0.15Candidate not overly broad
Stability0.15Holdout reproduced
Separation0.15Accused vs next best candidate

Unknown tests withdraw their weight; remaining weights renormalize. No ML. Same inputs → same score.

Publish threshold default: 0.50

Narration (LLM guard)

LLM receives a bundle of pre computed claims. Every number in prose is verified against the bundle. Unsupported numbers → template fallback. --no-llm changes wording only; zero number changes.

Known limitations (honest)

  • Baseline not validated by backtest yet → iOS 17.5 miss on unseen bundle
  • No trend model → slow drifts partially absorbed into baseline
  • Holdout is not a true out of sample test (same window used for selection)
  • No iterative peeling → one dominant cause can mask a smaller one
  • High cardinality IDs (app_id) outside lattice by design
  • Counterfactuals are accounting attribution, not causal proof of mechanism
